Our story

It started as a delivery route out of Muse.

Before there was a storefront there was a home ranch just outside LaBelle and a list of barns that needed hay. The stores came later, and the delivery trucks never stopped.

1998
The company is formed
Sweet Cypress Ranch, Inc. is registered with the State of Florida on October 20, 1998, with Al Curry of LaBelle as president.
Early 2000s
Hay and feed, delivered
Al Curry and Jo Carns run the business from their home in Muse. Deliveries make up the bulk of the business: hay and feed brought out and stacked in the customer's barn. One listing gives 2003 as the year the feed business was established.
Then
A storefront in Fort Myers, then LaBelle
Fort Myers comes first, for years on Palm Beach Boulevard as the S*C*R Hay, Feed & Trailer Warehouse. A second store follows on South Bridge Street in LaBelle. [OPENING YEARS]
2008
Will and Grace are born
A mare named Sue delivers twin foals at the ranch. Both survive, which almost never happens. Their story is below.
2010
A birthday party for the twins and the customers
Customer Appreciation Day at the ranch on January 30: a trail ride and poker run, an NBHA District 10 barrel race, and talks on choosing hay and feed from two feed companies.
Today
Two stores and the same delivery trucks
The Fort Myers store is now on Edison Avenue, LaBelle is still on South Bridge Street, and deliveries still run across Southwest Florida.

The people

Who you will be talking to.

The owners count more than 50 years of their own experience with horses and livestock, and they would rather talk through a feeding program than just ring up a bag. Ask about hay, minerals or supplements and you get an answer from someone who feeds animals at home.

Co-founder and president

Al Curry

Al runs daily operations at both stores and is the person to call about stock trailers. For 27 years he was also the announcer at the Cracker Day Rodeo at the Posse Arena in North Fort Myers, before handing over the microphone in 2023.

“There was cattle in Florida before there was cattle in Texas.”
Al Curry, at the 2022 Cracker Day Rodeo
Co-founder

Jo Carns

Jo teaches young riders and still runs barrels herself. At a March 2022 race held at the ranch she took first in her class on SCR Streakin Hotshot with a 16.815-second run. She is the one to ask about horse trailers and riding lessons.

In 2008 she and Al saw Grace, the smaller of the ranch's twin foals, through months of casts and surgery, and Jo wrote the updates that customers followed.

Office manager

Cyndi Skates Widener

A seventh-generation Floridian, Cyndi was introduced as the ranch's office manager in a 2017 Florida Weekly story on rodeo culture, and has long been a contact for the Fort Myers store and trailer sales.

Ranch history

Will and Grace.

In 2008 a mare named Sue foaled twins at the ranch. Twin foals rarely both live; one estimate puts the odds near one in 10,000 even when a mare carries them to term. These two came early, and Grace, the smaller at 45 pounds, was born with crooked legs that made standing a struggle.

Al and Jo chose to treat her. That meant weeks of casts, surgery and repeated trailer trips across the state to Dr. Bill Schachter's team at Wellington Equine. On May 6, 2008, the bandages came off and Grace ran free for the first time.

Will went on to work cattle as a ranch horse in Clewiston, then came home to retire. When the News-Press visited for their tenth birthday in 2018, Grace weighed 1,040 pounds, Will 1,224, and both were still at Sweet Cypress Ranch.

Heritage

Cattle country before it was anything else.

Florida has the longest ranching history of any state. Both towns the stores serve were built on it, and the work the stores supply today is the same work.

LaBelle

LaBelle began as a riverbank settlement of cattle drovers and trappers on the edge of Captain Francis A. Hendry's ranch. Family tradition holds that it was named for his daughters, Laura June and Carrie Belle.

Hendry was known as the Cattle King of South Florida. When the Legislature created a new county in 1923, it took his name and made LaBelle its seat. The town still holds the Swamp Cabbage Festival on the last full weekend of every February.

Fort Myers

When Thomas Edison arrived in 1885, Fort Myers was a small cow town of 349 people. Herds from inland ranches were driven down what is now First Street and McGregor Boulevard to the wharf at Punta Rassa and shipped to Cuba.

In 1872, about 18,000 of the roughly 21,000 Florida cattle sent to Cuba left from Punta Rassa. Cattlemen Jacob Summerlin and Captain Hendry both kept pens there.
